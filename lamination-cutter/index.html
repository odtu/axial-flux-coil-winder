Lamination Cutter

Fibre-laser SVGs for yokeless axial-flux teeth. Sheets stack outward in the radial direction, so every part is a plain rectangle and the tooth comes out as a trapezoidal prism with flat inner and outer faces.

02Lamination

With wire EDM every flank is cut on a true taper, so each sheet comes out at its exact width and width classes do not apply.

Adjacent sheets differ in width by less than the kerf, so cutting one per sheet is false precision. Grouping into classes collapses the part count and costs only a small flank error.

03Pole shoes

Integrated cuts each sheet as one I-shape, flanges included. The slot is then closed, so the coil has to go on by hand or pre-wound — the needle winder cannot reach in.

Separate shortens the body sheets and caps each end with its own plate, bonded on after winding. Keeps the needle winder usable.

Off: parallel flanks, so the coil can be wound straight onto the tooth with the winder.

04Process

Strips as wide as the tooth height are stacked to the radial length and clamped into one block. The wire runs through the stack and leans with each flank, so every sheet comes out at its exact width. Teeth alternate head-to-tail, so one cut can serve two teeth. Paths are wire-centre lines: set compensation to 0. Check your taper limit — many fast-wire machines manage ±6° as standard, more with a taper head.

Micro-tabs leave two small bridges of uncut steel per part, always on an edge with no job in the machine: the retention-tab tips, the flange tips, or the end faces. Outlines are offset outward by half the kerf. Digits are drawn as strokes, not text, so no font has to survive the trip into your laser software.

05Sheet

One tooth per file by default — cut it, move to fresh material, run it again. Width still wraps the rows; fit to the parts then trims the height so the SVG is a compact tile. Tooth height runs along sheet Y, so put the rolling direction on that axis.

06Retention frame

Every sheet gets a tab on both flanks at mid-height. Two identical frame halves drop on from each end of the tooth, trap the tabs between them and bolt together. The recesses are shallower than the tab by the clamp gap, so the faces never touch and the tabs take all the bolt load: no play. Wind each half of the coil afterwards, on its own frame face.

Off: plain rectangles. Hold the stack by bonding, or with a fixture of your own.

Output

Why the sheets stack outward

Flux in an axial-flux tooth runs along the machine axis, so the sheets only have to lie in planes containing that direction. Stacking them radially satisfies that and puts the tangential slot-leakage flux in the sheet plane too, rather than across it. Every sheet then lies in the tangential–axial plane, which makes it a plain rectangle.

Width classes

Tooth width grows linearly with radius, so neighbouring sheets differ by only 2·t·sin α — about 0.09 mm here, finer than the beam. Grouping sheets into classes of one width each cuts the part types from 77 to a handful; the flank error is half the width change across a class, and the average width keeps the iron area intact.

Grain direction

Non-oriented steel is still anisotropic. Flux runs along the tooth height, so h should lie along the rolling direction. Every part is placed with h on sheet Y and never rotated, which costs a little packing efficiency and buys lower loss.

Flat faces

Because each sheet is flat and perpendicular to the radius, the inner and outer faces come out as chords rather than arcs — the trapezoidal approximation. At r₂ = 72 mm with a 15° tooth that leaves 0.62 mm of iron on the table at the centre of the face, which is most of the gap between the fill figure and 100%.

Holding the teeth

The rotors pull each tooth along the axis — about 0.25 MPa of magnetic pressure at 0.8 T, and only from one side while the first rotor goes on. A tab is used rather than a groove because a groove takes steel out of the narrowest part of the tooth. The pull gets stronger as a tooth moves closer to the rotor, so any play grows instead of settling. That is why the frame clamps the tabs with no clearance at all.
