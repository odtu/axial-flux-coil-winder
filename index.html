Axial Coil Winder

Needle-winding G-code for axial-flux stators. The core sits on the bed; the hotend carries the wire guide and orbits each tooth in X‑Y, climbing Z one wire pitch per turn.

Setting the datum

      
Lift Z clear by hand, then G28 X Y — homing X and Y never moves Z on an Ender 3, so it is safe with the core clamped. Only G28 Z is dangerous. Touch the yoke face and G92 Z0; leave X and Y in true machine coordinates and let the alignment panel find the centre.

    

    

      
Two laps, two different jobs

      
The alignment lap is its own file: a marker in the guide, layer 1's path at the yoke face, where the standoff is tightest and a datum error shows up worst. The clearance lap runs before each tooth with the needle fitted, tracing the widest loop at the finished height — the one path that comes closest to the neighbours and the clamps. Both run at the check-lap feed rather than travel speed, so you can watch them and still reach the stop.

    

    

      
Where it gets tight

      
Slot pitch is smallest at the inner radius, so coils collide there first and the needle has to pass through whatever gap is left. The clearance figure is a true minimum over the whole path, not an estimate at one radius.

    

    

      
Wire twist

      
A needle winder puts one twist into the wire per turn, because the guide orbits and the spool does not. Feed from a spool on a swivel, or let a long free length above the guide absorb it.

    

    

      
Two different fill figures

      
Slot fill is the conventional one: both coil sides in a slot, over the slot area at the mean radius (slot width × tooth height). Bundle fill is how tightly the wire packs inside one coil's own cross-section — build × height — and depends on layer pitch, not on the machine. Coil build is measured normal to the tooth face, so it runs tangentially at the flanks and radially only at the two ends.

    

    

      
What the numbers assume

      
ρCu = 1.724×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 20 °C, scaled by 0.00393/K. Wire length is the summed needle path, so it runs slightly long against the true conductor centreline. Time ignores acceleration and is optimistic.
